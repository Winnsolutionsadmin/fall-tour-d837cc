The toggle reorders the board, and that is the finding. On cash, Plitvice wins at $5,800. On points, Warsaw drops to $3,818 and Kraków to $4,040 — because Flying Blue prices Kraków and Warsaw at the same Amsterdam-to-SFO award level with the European feeder free, so a cheap Ryanair city still buys a full transatlantic award. Poland has expensive cash exits and cheap award exits. That inversion is worth about $2,700.
The Kraków timing problem solved itself, and it pays you $345. Sunset there on Nov 1 is 16:19, so I assumed you had to choose between the candle-lit cemeteries and a sane flight. You do not. The Nov 1 20:30 departure and the Nov 2 10:15 departure are the same Copenhagen-to-SFO aircraft — identical arrival in San Francisco. Flying the next morning is $345 cheaper, cuts the journey from 27h20 to 13h35, and swaps a night in Copenhagen airport for a night in your own apartment. Stay for the whole evening.
Two corrections on Ireland. Ryanair does not only sell five Dublin seats on Oct 25 — driving the booking engine at true 5-passenger occupancy shows availability on Oct 25, 27, 28 and 30. Oct 25 is the cheapest, not the only. And the fare I had been quoting as "$705" was actually €705, which is $814 today, and rises to about $1,033 once you add three checked bags and the paid seat Ryanair forces beside an 8-year-old. The Derry lodging crisis is also narrower than feared: Oct 28–30 shows 24 available properties. It is exactly one night — Oct 31 — that is gone.
Lie-flat out of Dublin is effectively impossible. Aer Lingus has zero business award inventory to SFO across five checked dates, and cash for the same seats is $17,327 for two. Repositioning to Heathrow for Virgin Upper Class costs 287,500 points plus $5,500 in surcharges. If two flat beds matter more than the destination, Ireland is the wrong week — the flat-bed research pointed at Amsterdam and London instead.
