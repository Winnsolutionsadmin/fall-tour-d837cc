Executive Summary

Y5 — take CDG→SFO on AV LifeMiles, 10-30, 200,000 miles + $705. It is simultaneously the cheapest weighted total ($3,305), the highest-scoring candidate anywhere in the sweep (9/10), and the only build that pairs cheap verified positioning (Transavia DBV→ORY 10-29, $415/5, with 10-27 as a fallback) against a $0 tail — two flown segments total for a family of five, nonstop across the Atlantic, 9 seats of margin, and honest non-surcharge taxes.

F5 — take MUC→ORD, 10-27, 261,000 points + $2,434 (Aeroplan J ×2 + LifeMiles Y ×3). It beats the VIE hybrid on points, score, and inventory stability, and delivers the set's best redemption value (~3.0 cpp) — but understand what you are buying: two adults split across two aircraft and two PNRs with three kids, date-locked with zero slip room.

J5 — does not exist and should be struck from the plan. No origin in the sweep produced five business seats on one date; the deepest J inventory found anywhere was 5 seats decaying to 1 within 48 hours, and every route with real J depth was 1-stop.

The structural lesson across all three boards: the tail, not the award, decides the winner. Landing SFO ($0) or LAX ($310) versus ORD (~$900) or ATL ($2,017) swings total cost more than any points difference on the board — which is why five of the top six Y5 rows land at SFO and why the entire ORD block sits in the bottom two-thirds despite competitive award pricing.

Before transferring a single point, close the two open risks on the winner: re-pull CDG→SFO 10-30 award seats same-day (MR→LifeMiles is irreversible and this row was last confirmed on a cached snapshot), and book the ORY positioning ticket *only after* the award is ticketed — the two are separate tickets with no irrops protection, and a Transavia cancellation on 10-29 forfeits five award seats.
