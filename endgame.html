How the trip ends

Six ways to get from Dubrovnik to the Aer Lingus flight home. Same start, same finish — the difference is where you sleep, what you spend, and where you are on Halloween. Research resolved two things that reorder everything: Derry hosts Europe's largest Halloween festival, and Zagreb breaks the five-seat wall that Dubrovnik imposes.

Derry Halloween, Oct 28–31, is the find. Europe's largest Halloween festival, 40th anniversary, 100,000+ attendees — 70 miles from Dublin by coach. Your two target nights are its two best: the City of Bones illuminated walled-city trail on Oct 30 (£1 a head, a new 2026 crowd-control pilot) and the Carnival Parade at 7pm plus fireworks over the River Foyle at 8:15pm on Oct 31 — both free. There's a Little Horrors children's strand for the eight-year-old. One hard rule: there is no late-night transport back to Dublin on Oct 31, so if you watch the fireworks you sleep in Derry. Derry lodging for five on those nights books out months ahead and is the single biggest risk in this plan.
Three corrections from this pass. Dublin city has no Halloween parade — a 2024 AI-generated listing sent crowds to O'Connell Street for an event that never existed, and several "Dublin Halloween 2026" pages still repeat it. The Dubrovnik ferry is alive — my earlier Oct 18 scare was the wrong boat (TP Line 654 ends then; Krilo Star and TP Line 842 run daily through Oct 31), though it's a scenic option rather than a saving. And Split is a trap: Ryanair has zero departures from Split to Dublin, Stansted, Vienna or Bergamo in the entire window, and Aer Lingus has nothing to Split until April 2027. Split closes its Northern European network the day winter starts.
Lucca is blocked, not rejected. The 60th anniversary would be the highest-ceiling experience on the board for the teenagers — but tickets are in a dead window (early bird closed Jul 27, general sale opens Sept 1) and Lucca's historic centre is 99% booked for Oct 31–Nov 1, with a party of five effectively unavailable. Revisit in September if you want it.
The one number that isn't in any column. Every scenario ends on the same flight — Aer Lingus EI61, Dublin to San Francisco, 16,250 Avios per person plus $194.63 in fees, verified bookable with six or more seats open on every date. That leg is settled. Everything above is just how you get to it.
